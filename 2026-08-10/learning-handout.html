Vocabulary and grammar review

Extreme Weather and Present Tenses

Review the class vocabulary, choose a tense from meaning and time, and use always naturally in everyday speech.

1. Extreme weather and natural disasters

humidity

The amount of water vapour in the air. High humidity can make hot weather feel uncomfortable.

typhoon

A very powerful tropical storm in the western Pacific region.

snowstorm and blizzard

A snowstorm brings heavy snow. A blizzard is a severe snowstorm with strong winds and very poor visibility.

avalanche

A large mass of snow and ice that suddenly moves down a mountain.

central air-conditioning system

A system that cools and circulates air through a whole building.

2. Choose the present tense from the meaning

Present simple

Use it for routines and general facts.

Typhoons usually form over warm ocean water.

Present continuous

Use it for something happening now or around now, and for temporary situations.

The wind is getting stronger.

Present perfect

Use it for a past event or experience connected to now.

We have finished the Grammar Book practice.

3. Two different ways to use always

Regular habit

always + present simple

I always check the weather forecast before a trip.

This is a neutral routine.

Repeated behaviour with feeling

be always + -ing

He is always leaving the window open during storms.

This often adds annoyance, surprise, or emphasis.

The second pattern needs the correct form of be: am/is/are always + verb-ing.

4. Future learning list

  1. Expand and group extreme-weather and natural-disaster vocabulary.
  2. Choose the present simple, present continuous, or present perfect from short real-life situations.
  3. Contrast always + present simple with be always + -ing in natural speech.
  4. Give a focused weather answer: name the event, explain its effects, and add one preparation or safety response.
