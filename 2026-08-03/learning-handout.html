Grammar review

Sentence Roles and Passive Voice

Keep meaning roles clear, then use sentence position and time to build the correct voice.

1. Two kinds of labels

Meaning roles

Starter: the person or thing that begins the action.

Receiver: the person or thing affected by the action.

Grammar positions

Subject: the sentence position before the main verb.

Object: the position after an active verb.

Starter and receiver describe meaning. Subject and object describe grammar position. The meaning roles stay the same when active voice changes to passive voice.

2. Active and passive patterns

Active

starter + active verb + receiver

Passive

receiver + be + past participle + (by starter)

ActiveFamilies and schools shape shared values.

PassiveShared values are shaped by families and schools.

3. Position can change

ActiveI eat apples.

Subject: I · Verb: eat · Object: apples

PassiveApples are eaten by me.

The receiver, apples, moves into the subject position. The starter, I, becomes me after by.

4. Keep the time

The time expression controls the form of be. Do not change the time when you change the voice.

ActiveThousands of people shared the article last week.

PassiveThe article was shared by thousands of people last week.

General present: is shared / are shared

Past time: was shared / were shared

5. Build a passive sentence

  1. Name the starter and receiver.
  2. Move the receiver into the subject position.
  3. Keep the original time.
  4. Choose the correct form of be.
  5. Use the past participle.
  6. Add by + starter only when the doer is useful.

Vocabulary from class

Shared values are common beliefs or principles held by a group.
