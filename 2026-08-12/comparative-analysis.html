Comparative Analysis

Start by identifying what kind of text you are reading. In this class, we focused on the difference between fiction and non-fiction.

Today's starting point: identify the text type and its purpose before comparing details.

Fiction vs Non-fiction

Fiction

An imagined or shaped story

The writer may invent characters, events and settings. Fiction can still be inspired by real life.

Examples: romance, horror, fantasy, short stories and novels.

Common purposes: to entertain, explore an idea or create an emotional experience.

Non-fiction

Writing about real people, events or information

The writer presents facts, explanations, experiences or arguments. The writer's perspective still affects what is selected and emphasised.

Examples: news reports, documentaries, biographies and autobiographies.

Common purposes: to inform, explain, persuade or record experience.

The first comparison

Name the text typeIs each text fiction or non-fiction?
State the purposeWhat does each writer want the reader to understand, feel or believe?
Find one relationshipName one clear similarity or difference between the texts.
Useful sentence starter:
Both texts explore ________, but Text A ________, whereas Text B ________.

Quick check

Fiction: the story may be invented or shaped by imagination.

Non-fiction: the people, events or information are presented as real.

Comparative analysis: do more than describe two texts separately—show how they are similar or different.
