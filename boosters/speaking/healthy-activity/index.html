A Healthy Activity

Build a natural answer from a real routine, one personal moment and clear benefits.

Speaking goal
Give a connected answer for about 90 seconds. Add new meaning instead of repeating that the activity is “healthy” or “good”.

1. Key Ideas and a Useful Skill

Key Ideas

  • Follow every point on the task card, but connect the points as one story.
  • Use a real or realistic experience so you do not have to invent too much.
  • Explain one physical benefit and one mental or practical benefit.
  • Finish with the result: how the activity changes your day, health or mood.

Answer in four moves

1. Name itSay what the activity is and how often you do it.
2. Place itExplain where and with whom you do it.
3. Make it personalAdd one memory, difficulty or small change.
4. Explain the valueGive a body benefit, a mind benefit and a result.
Watch out: A list such as “It is healthy, relaxing and interesting” does not develop the answer. Add a reason, a real detail and a result.

2. Complete Model Answer

Annotation key: ACTIVITY names the topic · ROUTINE gives setting · PERSONAL DETAIL makes it real · BENEFIT explains value · RESULT closes the answer.

ACTIVITYI’m going to talk about cycling, which is the healthy activity I enjoy most. I usually go for a ride once or twice a week, especially when I have been sitting at a desk for a long time.

ROUTINEI normally cycle along a quiet path beside the river near my home. I often go alone because I can choose my own speed, although one of my friends joins me during the holidays.

PERSONAL DETAILWhen I first started, I became tired after about twenty minutes. I decided not to ride too fast and increased the distance little by little. After a few weeks, I could complete the whole route without stopping, which made me feel proud.

BENEFITCycling is useful for my health because it improves my fitness and helps me build strength in my legs. It also gives me a chance to switch off. While I am riding, I focus on the road, the weather and my breathing instead of schoolwork or other worries.

RESULTAs a result, I usually return home feeling refreshed and more energetic. That combination of exercise and quiet thinking is the main reason I have made cycling part of my weekly routine.

What to Notice

Underline the sentence that shows how the speaker’s ability changed over time.
Circle one physical benefit and box one mental benefit.
Which personal detail makes the answer sound like an experience rather than a memorised list?

4. Ideas for Your Answer

Choose one route. Add your own personal detail before you speak.

Cycling: steady progress

Central idea: a regular ride is simple exercise.

Development: begin with a short route → increase the distance gradually → notice better fitness and confidence.

Concrete detail: the first time you completed a hill or a longer route without stopping.

Useful expressions: go for a ride · increase the distance little by little · build strength · feel refreshed

Swimming: focus and control

Central idea: swimming trains the whole body without feeling rushed.

Development: practise one stroke → control breathing → improve stamina → leave the pool calmer.

Concrete detail: a lesson, a difficult length or the first time you swam continuously.

Useful expressions: practise my technique · control my breathing · improve my stamina · clear my mind

Walking: a manageable habit

Central idea: walking fits easily into a busy week.

Development: choose a regular route → walk with someone or listen carefully to the surroundings → reduce stress.

Concrete detail: a conversation, a change in the weather or a day when the walk improved your mood.

Useful expressions: make time for a walk · keep a steady pace · take a break from screens · return home feeling calmer

Your personal detail

Where were you?

Who was with you?

What was difficult at first?

What can you do now that you could not do before?

Choose one route and reduce it to four keywords. Do not write full sentences.

5. Useful Expressions

Describe a routine

Use: show that the activity is part of your life.

Model: I have made cycling part of my weekly routine.

Changed detail: I have made an evening walk part of my Friday routine.

Watch out: change the activity and frequency so the sentence stays true.

Show improvement

Use: turn a description into a small story.

Model: I increased the distance little by little.

Changed detail: I increased the number of lengths little by little.

Watch out: say what increased.

Explain a physical benefit

Use: give a precise health reason.

Model: It helps me build strength in my legs.

Changed detail: It helps me improve my stamina.

Watch out: use helps me + verb, not helps me to improving.

Explain a mental benefit

Use: show how the activity changes your mood.

Model: It gives me a chance to switch off.

Changed detail: It gives me a chance to clear my mind after school.

Watch out: explain what you stop thinking about or how you feel afterwards.

6. Your Speaking Turn

Describe something healthy you enjoy doing.

You should say:

  • what you do
  • where you do it
  • who you do it with

and explain why you think doing this is healthy.

Cover the model and the idea bank. Write only four keywords for your answer.
Give your first answer for 60–75 seconds. Do not stop to correct every small mistake.
Choose one improvement: add a clearer personal detail, explain one benefit more precisely, or repair one repeated sentence. Write the change in one sentence.
Answer again for about 90 seconds. Keep the four-keyword plan, but include your improved sentence naturally.

7. Key Vocabulary

stamina — the ability to continue physical effort · improve my stamina
technique — the way a physical skill is performed · practise my technique
self-discipline — control that helps you continue a useful habit · develop self-discipline
refreshed — less tired and more energetic · return home feeling refreshed
steady pace — a regular, controlled speed · keep a steady pace
