Nelson | Writing Task 2 UnitOpinion Essay

Community Service at School

Build a clear position, give each body paragraph one job, and develop ideas with reasons and examples.

Writing goal
Plan and write a complete opinion essay in which every paragraph has one clear central topic and each main idea is developed.

1. The Task

Some people think that all secondary-school students should be required to do unpaid community service in their free time.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

What is the topic, what type of question is this, and what position will the model take?

2. Key Ideas and a Useful Skill

Key Ideas

  • Decide your position before you collect vocabulary.
  • Give each body paragraph one central topic.
  • Number the ideas before writing so the paragraph has a clear direction.
  • Develop an idea with an explanation, a concrete example and a result or limitation.

Plan in four moves

1. IdentifyTopic: school community service. Question: opinion.
2. DecideClear position: useful when voluntary, not when compulsory.
3. SeparateBody 1: problems with force. Body 2: benefits of choice.
4. NumberCentral idea → explanation → example → result.
Watch out: Do not put three unrelated benefits into one paragraph. Select one central topic and develop it fully.

3. Complete Model Essay

Annotation key: POSITION answers the question · CENTRAL IDEA gives the paragraph one job · EXPLANATION shows why · EXAMPLE makes it concrete · RESULT shows the effect.

TOPICMany people believe that every secondary-school student should complete unpaid work for the local community during their free time. POSITIONI agree that schools should offer community-service opportunities, but I do not believe that every student should be forced to take part.

CENTRAL IDEAThe main problem with compulsory service is that students have very different demands on their time. EXPLANATIONSome teenagers are preparing for important examinations, while others travel long distances to school or help their families at home. EXAMPLEFor example, a sixteen-year-old who studies in the evening and looks after a younger brother may not be able to spend every Saturday on a school project. RESULTIf that student is forced to participate, the activity may feel like a punishment rather than a useful contribution. As a result, the student may do the work carelessly and become less willing to volunteer in the future.

CENTRAL IDEAVoluntary programmes, by contrast, can give motivated students valuable experience. EXPLANATIONWhen young people select a project that matches their interests, they are more likely to take responsibility and communicate well with other people. EXAMPLEA school could, for instance, work with a local community centre and allow students to help older residents use computers for two hours each month. RESULTThe residents would receive practical support, while the students would develop patience, confidence and communication skills. This kind of experience could also strengthen students’ future applications for further study or work.

CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, community service can benefit both students and local people, but schools should encourage it through choice and good organisation instead of making it compulsory for everyone.

5. Writing Structure and Ideas for Your Answer

Route A: Choice improves commitment

Central idea: voluntary work attracts students who want to contribute.

Development: personal choice → greater responsibility → better-quality work.

Concrete example: students choose between an environmental project and helping at a community centre.

Result: students continue because they see a real effect.

Useful expressions: choose a project that matches their interests · take responsibility for · make a useful contribution · see the effect of their work

Route B: Service develops practical skills

Central idea: well-designed projects teach abilities that classrooms cannot always provide.

Development: work with different people → solve a real problem → communicate clearly.

Concrete example: teach basic computer skills to older residents.

Result: greater patience, confidence and work readiness.

Useful expressions: gain valuable experience · develop communication skills · work with people of different ages · prepare students for adult responsibilities

Route C: Compulsion ignores real pressures

Central idea: one fixed requirement does not fit every student.

Development: examinations, travel or family duties → limited free time → stress or poor participation.

Concrete example: a student who studies and cares for a younger family member.

Result: community work feels like punishment and motivation falls.

Useful expressions: place extra pressure on · have different demands on their time · be forced to take part · become less willing to volunteer

Your missing choice

Which route will control Body 1?

Which different route will control Body 2?

What example will you personalise?

What limitation will you acknowledge?

Choose two different routes. Write one central-topic sentence for each body paragraph, then add one personalised example.

6. Useful Expressions

State a balanced position

Model: I agree that schools should offer these opportunities, but I do not believe participation should be compulsory.

Changed detail: I support financial education at school, but I do not believe it should replace academic subjects.

Watch out: the two sides of the sentence must not contradict your essay plan.

Explain a cause

Model: The main problem is that students have different demands on their time.

Changed detail: One benefit is that students can gain experience outside the classroom.

Watch out: explain the claim in the next sentence; do not move to a new topic.

Give a concrete example

Model: A school could, for instance, work with a local community centre.

Changed detail: Students could, for example, organise a monthly park-cleaning project.

Watch out: use an example that directly proves the central idea.

Show a result

Model: As a result, the student may become less willing to volunteer.

Changed detail: As a result, participants may become more confident when speaking to adults.

Watch out: name who experiences the result.

8. Key Vocabulary

compulsory — required by a rule · compulsory participation
voluntary — chosen freely · a voluntary programme
contribution — work that helps a group or activity · make a useful contribution
commitment — willingness to give time and effort · show commitment to a project
community centre — a local place offering activities or support · help at a community centre
responsibility — a duty or the ability to act reliably · take responsibility for a task
