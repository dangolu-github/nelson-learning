3. Key vocabulary

identityThe qualities, experiences and groups that shape how a person understands who they are.Language can be part of a person’s identity.
shared identityA sense of belonging based on experiences, values, language, place or history that people have in common.A shared identity can connect people from different backgrounds.
nationalityA person’s legal or national membership.Nationality can influence identity, but it is not the whole of identity.
immigrationThe movement of people into another country to live there.Immigration can bring several languages and traditions into one family.
generation gapA difference in attitudes or experiences between younger and older people.A generation gap may affect how family identity is understood.
stereotypeA fixed, oversimplified belief about a group.One viral post can strengthen a stereotype.
biased viewAn opinion influenced unfairly by one side, limited information or prior beliefs.A biased view may ignore evidence that does not fit it.
objectiveBased on facts and fair consideration rather than personal feelings.An objective explanation compares more than one source.
evidenceInformation or examples used to support a claim.A strong claim is supported by relevant evidence.
assumption / preconceptionA belief accepted before enough evidence is checked. Preconception is more natural than pre-assumption in academic English.Question the assumption before accepting the claim.
equalityThe principle that people should have equal rights, value or opportunities.Equality can be a shared social value.
democracyA system in which people take part in choosing representatives or making public decisions.Democracy may be discussed as part of national identity.

4. Use neutral academic language

Instead of That is weird, say:

  • This view may be based on limited evidence.
  • This description seems oversimplified.
  • The claim may reflect a stereotype.
  • A more objective explanation would compare several sources.
